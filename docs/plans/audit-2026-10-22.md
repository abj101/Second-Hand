# Avatar harness audit, 2026-10-22 (HEAD `a017ba2`, after r11)

**Summary (6 lines)**
1. **Verdict: working but fragile.** The loop got a party from boot (LV11, truth step 104), but 36 of 150 steps were route failures, the lab exit was never found, and the rival battle did not finish. The suite is green (755/1) and protects code shape more than behaviour.
2. **Gap 1: modal screens write world memory.** Battle, keyboard and dialogue perceptions get world tiles. In LV11, 41 of the lab's 66 memory rows are HP bars, keyboard keys and menu items. Real rows were renamed ("e_player" became "Charmander HP"), and 29 of the 43 act options at step 117 were keyboard keys.
3. **Gap 2: a failure is filed under the wrong cause.** Every LV11 exit failure (36) happened at the route stage, before the agent reached the exit. O1 keys each failure by push direction, so the act model read four route failures as "UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT all failed". The crossing-stage machinery from E3–E5 never ran.
4. **Gap 3: unverified perception drives navigation, and routes cross unseen ground silently.** The lab "door (top wall)" is a wall decoration at screen (0.15, 0.15); the real exit is at the bottom edge, off screen. Routes through unseen cells fail one cell at a time, and nothing in a route's label says it crosses unseen ground.
5. **Blocking the next milestone:** (a) memory corruption after the first battle and keyboard screen; (b) leaving a room whose exit is off screen, with phantom exits in the way; (c) battles: RUN was picked twice after the game refused it, and one battle turn takes 3 steps (about 7 asks); (d) Route 1 to Viridian needs long walks, where a 24% route-failure rate compounds.
6. **Measurement:** RAM-truth gates (party step, maps) are trustworthy. A3 is not: 4 of its 8 PASS cases pass by construction, and dry mode never tests a choice. `m1_gate_pass` is half self-referential. MC2 is one sample per case. Run provenance reads "complete" on a dirty tree, and the committed caps (4815) cannot run against the ledger (7304).

Evidence lives in `.tmp/audit/` (lv11/, gates/prompts.txt, tests/, hygiene/protected.diff, evals/a3.txt). The audit made no model requests, and the ledger is unchanged at 7304 / 7854.

### A2. Route failures are filed as push failures (high; decision information; P1, P4)
- **Evidence**
  - LV11 had 36 route-blocked steps and 0 crossing-stage failures.
  - The lab door rows show `travel: leg 1 route blocked 1×` separately under UP (step 88), DOWN (92) and LEFT (116), while RIGHT stays "new".
  - The act model's why at 116 (`.tmp/r11/report.md:75`): "since pressing UP and DOWN failed, I will try pressing LEFT or RIGHT". None of those pushes was ever pressed.
  - E3–E5 never fired, because `failed_push` is only called at the crossing stage (`loop.py:281`).
- **Root cause**
  - O1 makes the push part of the intent key for every failure stage (`world.py:1018`; `loop.py:189`, `:211`).
  - A route that stops short says nothing about the push, yet it is charged to it.
  - The label offers a press choice before the exit tile has ever been reached.
- **Options**
  - **A:** key route-stage failures on the exit ref without `>DIR`. All direction rows then share one note, "not reached: blocked at (x,y) n×". Size: about 10 lines, 0 asks.
  - **B (recommended):**
    - Until the player has stood on the exit tile, an uncrossed exit is one row, `go to <exit>`, and its failures are route failures.
    - Standing on it, the rows are the push directions, with F1's facts.
    - This gives fewer rows (4 → 1 per perceived exit), honest notes, and the push choice where it means something.
    - Size: about 25 lines in `World.options`, plus the O1/F1 test classes (pre-approval needed), 0 asks.
  - **C:** keep, and add a note text only. This leaves the clutter.

### A3. Phantom exits and silent routes through unseen ground (high; world model, decision; P1, P4)
- **Evidence**
  - The lab "exit: door" was perceived at screen (0.15, 0.15), top left, as "top wall". Truth puts the lab exit on the bottom edge: `m1_exit_b1_live_150` steps 139, 141 and 147, DOWN from (5,11). That door was picked 10 times between steps 87 and 123.
  - Outdoors, 6 perceived "path (top wall)" exits along the fence were each tried and refuted one at a time (steps 54–60).
  - `eval_moves` records 26 failed rows with the cause "unknown_cell".
  - The step-55 route ran into `e_fence_post_9`, which was known as a static thing but stored one tile off.
- **Root cause**
  - `space.route` (`space.py:54`) treats every unknown cell as passable, and option labels never say so. The system chooses a path through unseen ground without telling the agent.
  - `_refuted` (`world.py:234`) counts only a blocked push *into* the exit tile. A target that is unreachable from every side is never recorded as such.
  - Perceived placement is trusted for exits that have no tile evidence.
- **Options**
  - **A (recommended):** facts, not gates.
    - The route's label states how many of its tiles are unseen: `go to door (top wall), 6 left, 2 up, 4 of 8 steps on unseen ground`.
    - A route failure toward a target is written to memory on that target, `unreached: blocked at (x,y)`, and shown on every row that targets it.
    - Size: about 20 lines, 0 asks.
  - **B:** prefer routes over known floor and fall back to unknown cells; tell the agent which one it got. This is more code and still a hidden choice.
  - **C:** let the perceiver re-check an exit (zoom on the exit point) before it becomes a row. Costs 1 ask per new exit; it attacks perception and does not fix the false-fact problem.

### A4. Game replies to menu choices are not remembered on the choice (high for battles; decision; P4)

- **Options**
  - **A (recommended):** a modal select row gets a note `chosen step s: game said "<first new line>"`, keyed by screen place plus name (no entity id after A1-B). Size: about 10 lines, 0 asks.
  - **B:** treat "same screen + text appeared + menu returned" as failed. That is a mechanical judgement of the reply; not recommended (P1).

### Amendments after the user's questions (2026-10-22, pending)

**How it works today (checked in code):**
- **Routing** is already mechanical.
  - `space.route` runs a BFS over the place's tile memory (`space.py:40-56`), and `World.plan` chains legs across places (`world.py:1212`).
  - The act model only picks a target ("go to X", "explore DIR").
  - It gets stuck because of the **map it routes on**, not the search:
    - Passability is known only for tiles walked (`floor`) and sides bumped (`blocked`, `space.py:9-30`), plus perceived static things. Every other cell, including a wall in plain view, counts as passable. At r11 step 112 the lab had 25 walked cells, 194 "seen" cells and no passability for the other 169.
    - A route stops at the first bump (`skills.py:96`) and the whole step ends. The act model then re-picks, so each wall costs a full step (2–4 asks). 36 of 150 LV11 steps ended this way.
    - ~~The believed position drifts on failed routes~~ **Withdrawn after P0:** the offset to truth is constant through r11 steps 86–125. Across 16 runs there are only 16 drift events in 2,381 steps, each about 1 cell, and 14 of them came on dialogue advances. The earlier claim compared against the truth row *before* the step.
    - Targets are wrong: phantom exits, and things stored one tile off (the step-55 fence post).
- **"Seen" and "unseen":** `_record_view` (`world.py:260-275`) marks every cell inside the screen rectangle as `seen` and stores nothing about what is in it. "Unseen" means never on screen. Things the perceiver lists do get a tile without walking (an NPC, an item, a fence post), but the ground itself does not: walls, tables, water and grass are unknown until they are walked on or bumped.
- **Menus and the keyboard** are re-read from scratch every time.
  - `select` asks a model for the menu layout on every selection (`skills.py:190-205`); `type_text` asks for the keyboard layout (`skills.py:235-285`).
  - What a choice led to is never stored (A4).
  - Exact-frame perceiver cache hits already save a lot (98 of 196 LV11 perceiver calls), but nothing is learned about a screen's layout.
